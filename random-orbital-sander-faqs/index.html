Random Orbital Sander questions answered

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

Random orbital sanders are your best bet for a smooth, swirl-free finish because the motion is truly random. They're beginner-friendly, versatile for wood and paint prep, and come in a few common sizes. Focus on pad size, dust collection, and variable speed to get the right tool for your projects.

What is a random orbital sander used for?

A random orbital sander is designed for fine finishing work on wood, metal, plastic, and painted surfaces. It excels at removing light material, smoothing joints, and preparing surfaces for staining or painting. Because of its gentle, random motion, it leaves almost no swirl marks, making it the go-to tool for any project where appearance matters. That said, it is not a heavy stock removal tool. If you need to quickly take down a lot of material, you’ll want a belt sander or angle grinder instead. A random orbital sander is all about control and finesse.

How does a random orbital sander work?

Inside the sander, the sanding pad spins on its own center while also moving in a small offset circle, an orbit. The combination of these two motions is never exactly repeated, so each sanding grit touches the surface from a slightly different angle. That randomness is what eliminates the telltale parallel scratches you see from a regular palm sander. Many models also let you adjust the orbit diameter (the size of that offset circle). A larger orbit removes material faster but leaves a slightly coarser finish; a smaller orbit is slower but smoother. For most woodworking, a medium orbit around 3/32 of an inch strikes a good balance.

What grit sandpaper should I start with?

The grit you start with depends on the condition of your surface. For bare wood that’s already fairly smooth, begin with 120 or 150 grit. For removing old paint or leveling a rough board, you might start as low as 60 or 80 grit. Always progress in steps, jump no more than about 50 grit numbers at a time (e.g., 80, then 120, then 180). After your final pass with 220 or 320 grit, the surface will be silky smooth. For painted surfaces, you can go even finer, up to 400 or 600 grit, for a high-gloss finish. Stick with quality aluminum oxide or ceramic discs, cheap paper clogs fast and leaves disappointing results.

Which pad size is better: 5-inch or 6-inch?

Five-inch sanders are lighter, more maneuverable, and easier on the wrist for extended overhead work or tight spots. They also use smaller discs, which are cheaper and more widely available. A 5-inch sander is the standard choice for furniture builders and finish carpenters. Six-inch sanders cover more surface area per pass, so they speed up work on large flat panels like tabletops or cabinet doors. However, they are heavier and can feel clumsy in corners. For a home workshop that does a mix of projects, a 5-inch sander is usually the more practical pick. If you mostly refinish large tabletops, consider a 6-inch.

What features matter most when buying?

Variable speed is essential, it lets you slow down for heat-sensitive materials like plastic or veneer, and crank up for aggressive stock removal. Good dust collection is also critical: look for a sander with a dust port that fits your shop vac hose, or at least a well-designed dust canister that captures most of the fine particles. A pad brake quickly stops the pad when you lift the sander, preventing accidental swirls when you set it back down. Ergonomic soft-grip handles and a low vibration rating make long sessions comfortable. Finally, check the vibration rating: lower numbers (around 2 to 3 m/s²) mean less fatigue.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between a random orbital and a finishing sander?

A traditional finishing sander (or palm sander) moves in a fixed, repeating orbit, which often leaves visible swirl marks. A random orbital sander’s motion is truly random, it never repeats the same path, so the finish is swirl-free. Random orbital sanders also tend to be more versatile and easier to control.

Can I use a random orbital sander for wet sanding?

Most random orbital sanders are not designed for wet sanding. Moisture can get into the bearings and motor, causing rust or electrical shorts. If you need to wet-sand, look for a model specifically rated for wet use, or use a waterproof sanding block by hand.

Do I need a dust collector for a random orbital sander?

Yes, strongly recommended. Random orbital sanders generate a huge amount of very fine dust that’s harmful to breathe and coats your shop. Most models come with a dust port that connects to a shop vac. A dedicated dust extractor with a HEPA filter is even better for health and to keep the sander’s pad from clogging.

How do I choose the right sandpaper for a random orbital sander?

Use hook-and-loop (Velcro) backed discs in the correct diameter for your pad. Choose grit based on your task: coarse for removal, medium for smoothing, fine for finishing. Stick to high-quality aluminum oxide or ceramic discs, they last longer and cut more evenly than cheap alternatives. Also match the hole pattern of the disc to your sander’s dust collection holes.

What speed should I set for sanding wood?

For aggressive stock removal, use the highest speed setting. For final finishing or sanding veneer, lower the speed to around 6,000 to 8,000 orbits per minute. This reduces heat buildup and prevents burning the wood or damaging the surface. Many variable-speed sanders have a numbered dial, start in the middle and adjust as you go.

Are random orbital sanders loud?

They produce a moderate noise level, typically between 75 and 85 decibels, enough to cause hearing damage over extended periods. Always wear hearing protection when sanding for more than a few minutes. Some premium models are noticeably quieter due to better sound dampening.

In shortRemember, a random orbital sander is a forgiving tool that rewards patience and good technique. Invest in quality discs, use a dust collector, and let the sander do the work, your projects will look professional and you’ll enjoy the process far more.
